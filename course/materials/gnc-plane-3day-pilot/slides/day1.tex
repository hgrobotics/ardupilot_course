\section{Module M1 --- Overview \& Ecosystem (1.0 h, survey)}

\begin{frame}[allowframebreaks]{Module M1 --- ArduPilot Overview \& Ecosystem}
  \textbf{1.0 h, lecture+demo, \emph{survey} depth.}\\[1ex]
  Learning objectives:
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item Place ArduPilot in the open-source autopilot landscape; recognise the GPLv3 posture and resulting adoption constraints for proprietary use.
    \item Recognise the 6-vehicle / shared-libraries / HAL architecture in the directory tree.
    \item Locate ArduPlane's main vehicle class and scheduler table; confirm the \textbf{50 Hz default fast-loop} for plane vs Copter's 400 Hz default --- and \emph{why}.
    \item Recognise that Lua and DDS/ROS2 exist as integration surfaces; they live in the 5-day source, not in this pilot.
  \end{enumerate}
  \insttiming{1.0 h survey. 8--10 slides max. Watch-only demo at the end ($\sim$5 min).}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{The architecture in one sentence}
  ArduPilot is a multi-vehicle autopilot built as \textbf{vehicles + shared libraries + a HAL}.
  \vfill
  \begin{itemize}
    \item Vehicles: \codepath{ArduPlane/}, \codepath{ArduCopter/}, \codepath{Rover/}, \codepath{ArduSub/}, \codepath{AntennaTracker/}, \codepath{Blimp/} --- each inherits from \texttt{AP\_Vehicle} and registers a scheduler table.
    \item Shared libraries: $\sim$150. Naming prefixes are load-bearing: \texttt{AP\_*} general, \texttt{AC\_*} Copter-flavoured, \texttt{AR\_*} Rover-flavoured, \texttt{APM\_Control} plane-specific attitude PIDs.
    \item HAL: \codepath{libraries/AP\_HAL/} defines the platform-independent interface; concrete impls live in \codepath{AP\_HAL\_ChibiOS}, \codepath{AP\_HAL\_Linux}, \codepath{AP\_HAL\_ESP32}, \codepath{AP\_HAL\_SITL}.
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}[allowframebreaks]{Plane class anchor --- one cite}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item \citerange{ArduPlane/Plane.h}{1}{50} --- \texttt{class Plane : public AP\_Vehicle} declaration.
  \end{itemize}
  \vfill
  Open it once. Notice the \texttt{public AP\_Vehicle} inheritance --- the same parent every other vehicle inherits from.
  \instnote{Do not click into the file on the projector unless asked. Engineers should open in their own editors.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}[allowframebreaks]{The Plane fast-loop is 50 Hz --- and \emph{why}}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item \citerange{ArduPlane/Plane.cpp}{62}{95} --- \texttt{scheduler\_tasks[]}: \texttt{FAST\_TASK(ahrs\_update)}, \texttt{FAST\_TASK(stabilize)}, \texttt{FAST\_TASK(set\_servos)}, then rate-limited \texttt{SCHED\_TASK(...)} entries.
    \item \citerange{libraries/AP\_Scheduler/AP\_Scheduler.cpp}{43}{49} --- \texttt{SCHEDULER\_DEFAULT\_LOOP\_RATE} selects 400 under \texttt{APM\_BUILD\_COPTER\_OR\_HELI}, \textbf{50 otherwise}.
    \item \citerange{libraries/AP\_Scheduler/AP\_Scheduler.cpp}{55}{69} --- \texttt{SCHED\_LOOP\_RATE} parameter, \texttt{@Range: 50 400}, \texttt{@RebootRequired: True}.
  \end{itemize}
  \vfill
  Why 50 Hz: fixed-wing dynamics are an order of magnitude slower than a multirotor's. The Nyquist budget is comfortable at 50 Hz for attitude and far more comfortable for guidance.
  \instfaq{Why is the rate static at compile time?}{Compile-time selection lets the linker drop the high-rate path entirely from the plane binary. Runtime override exists via SCHED\_LOOP\_RATE; reboot required.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}[allowframebreaks]{Compare to your stack}
  Most proprietary stacks have an equivalent of the scheduler table.
  \vfill
  The question worth thinking about:
  \begin{quote}
    Is yours statically declared at compile time (ArduPilot) or built up at runtime?
  \end{quote}
  \vfill
  ArduPilot's static table is small, fast, and inspectable. It costs you the ability to add a task without recompiling.
  \instadvanced{The 5-day source (\siblingplane) covers Lua scripting in M13 as the runtime task-injection escape hatch. Out of scope for this pilot.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Library naming carries meaning --- one printed handout}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item \citerange{course/custom\_gnc\_course\_plane.md}{1280}{1295} --- Copter$\leftrightarrow$Plane library mapping table from Appendix C of the 5-day source. Printed handout.
  \end{itemize}
  \vfill
  Recognition only. We will return to specific libraries (\texttt{AP\_HAL}, \texttt{AP\_Param}, \texttt{AP\_NavEKF3}, \texttt{AP\_TECS}, \texttt{AP\_L1\_Control}, \texttt{APM\_Control}) at internals depth in M4--M8.
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}[allowframebreaks]{GPLv3 and the adoption constraint --- state it once, move on}

  \vfill
  Vendoring a subsystem into a proprietary codebase carries license obligations. The legal posture is your organisation's call --- not the course's.
  \vfill
  We state this here, return to it explicitly in M10, and do not relitigate it elsewhere. The capstone you take home is technically working extracted code; the legal review is yours to run separately.
  \instnote{Do not editorialise on the GPLv3 question. The audience has lawyers; we have not. State, move on.}
\end{frame}

\section{Module M2 --- Operations Essentials, compressed (1.5 h, applied)}

\begin{frame}[allowframebreaks]{Module M2 --- Operations Essentials, compressed}
  \textbf{1.5 h, lecture+demo, \emph{applied} depth.}\\[1ex]
  Learning objectives:
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item Launch ArduPlane SITL from a clean clone, attach MAVProxy, take off in \flmode{TAKEOFF}, switch to \flmode{FBWA}, fly a heading, switch to \flmode{RTL}.
    \item Read MAVProxy's textual telemetry: altitude, airspeed, mode, heartbeat.
    \item Recognise plane-specific dataflash messages: \param{ATT}, \param{CTUN}, \param{NTUN}, \param{ARSP}, \param{TECS}.
  \end{enumerate}
  \vfill
  We are \emph{not} teaching mission planning, the GCS UI, or geofence/rally setup. You already operate a proprietary autopilot.
  \insttiming{1.5 h applied. $\sim$10 min code-along at the end. M4 carries the first formal lab.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}[allowframebreaks]{The launch sequence --- the canonical SITL launcher}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item[\$] \texttt{Tools/autotest/sim\_vehicle.py -v ArduPlane -f plane --console --map}
  \end{itemize}
  Two cite anchors into the launcher's argparse:
  \begin{itemize}
    \item \citeline{Tools/autotest/sim\_vehicle.py}{1} --- file header.
    \item \citerange{Tools/autotest/sim\_vehicle.py}{1073}{1240} --- build + sim option groups: \texttt{--vehicle}, \texttt{--frame}, \texttt{--debug} (line 1106), \texttt{--gdb} (line 1213).
    \item \citerange{Tools/autotest/sim\_vehicle.py}{1405}{1436} --- MAVProxy GUI option group: \texttt{--map} (line 1413), \texttt{--console} (line 1422).
  \end{itemize}
  \vfill
  Argparse is split into named option groups --- a useful Python idiom in itself. We will use \texttt{--debug} in L1 (M4) and \texttt{--gdb} in M9.
  \instnote{This cite was the iter-1 blocker location. The plan-iter-2 cite ranges (1073--1240, 1405--1436) honestly anchor the build/sim and MAVProxy GUI groups; do not revert to \texttt{:1500-1600}.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{TAKEOFF is software, not a hardware mode}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item \citerange{ArduPlane/mode\_takeoff.cpp}{1}{80} --- \texttt{ModeTakeoff} is a \texttt{Mode} subclass with \texttt{update()}, \texttt{verify\_takeoff()}, configuration parameters.
  \end{itemize}
  \vfill
  There is no special path through the firmware for takeoff. The mode runs the same control pipeline, parametrised differently.
  \vfill
  Internalise this: every ArduPilot flight mode is a class that overrides \texttt{update()} (and a few other hooks) on the \texttt{Mode} base. No compile-time mode dispatch.
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}[allowframebreaks]{FBWA in 45 lines --- read aloud}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item \citerange{ArduPlane/mode\_fbwa.cpp}{1}{45} --- the entire \texttt{ModeFBWA} body. The file is exactly 45 lines.
  \end{itemize}
  \vfill
  \texttt{ModeFBWA::update()} clamps stick inputs to attitude limits and writes desired roll and pitch into \texttt{nav\_roll\_cd} and \texttt{nav\_pitch\_cd}. That is the entire mode. The actual stabilisation runs in \texttt{Plane::stabilize} (called from the fast-loop scheduler table).
  \vfill
  Note the unit: \texttt{*\_cd} is \textbf{centi-degrees} --- the canonical integer angle unit in ArduPilot vehicle code, signed \texttt{int32\_t}.
  \instfaq{Why centi-degrees?}{Historic --- predates pervasive \texttt{float} on flight hardware. The unit is now baked into MAVLink and into log structure layouts; renaming is forbidden by ABI compatibility.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Compare to your stack --- mode dispatch}
  Most proprietary stacks dispatch flight-mode behaviour through a dedicated state machine.
  \vfill
  ArduPilot pushes the dispatch into virtual methods on the \texttt{Mode} base.
  \vfill
  Trade-off:
  \begin{itemize}
    \item \emph{Cost}: dynamic-dispatch (one vtable indirection) on every fast-loop tick.
    \item \emph{Gain}: ``mode = polymorphic object'' is conceptually compact and adding a new mode is a new \texttt{.cpp} file.
  \end{itemize}
  \vfill
  The cost is bounded; the readability gain is large.
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}[allowframebreaks]{Plane-specific log messages --- recognition only}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item \param{ATT} --- attitude (\texttt{Roll}, \texttt{Pitch}, \texttt{Yaw}, \texttt{DesRoll}, \texttt{DesPitch}, \texttt{DesYaw})
    \item \param{CTUN} --- control tuning (throttle output, target altitude vs barometric)
    \item \param{NTUN} --- nav tuning (L1 lateral state)
    \item \param{ARSP} --- airspeed (raw, calibrated, EAS$\leftrightarrow$TAS scale)
    \item \param{TECS} --- energy controller state (\texttt{h}, \texttt{hdem}, \texttt{dh}, \texttt{dhdem}, \texttt{spdem}, \texttt{sp})
    \item \param{XKF1}--\param{XKF5} --- EKF3 internals
  \end{itemize}
  \vfill
  L4 leans on \param{ATT} and \param{TECS}; L3 leans on \param{XKF*}. The full reference list is at \citerange{course/custom\_gnc\_course\_plane.md}{130}{138} (printed handout).
  \instnote{Do not unpack each message. Recognition only --- M9 covers log layout at applied depth.}
\end{frame}

\section{Module M3 --- Build System \& Dev Environment (1.0 h, applied)}

\begin{frame}{Module M3 --- Build System \& Dev Environment}
  \textbf{1.0 h, lecture+lab, \emph{applied} depth.}\\[1ex]

    \item Run \texttt{./waf configure --board sitl --debug \&\& ./waf plane}. Locate the artifact at \codepath{build/sitl/bin/arduplane}.

  \end{enumerate}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Waf in three lines}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item \texttt{./waf configure --board sitl --debug} --- writes \codepath{build/sitl/c4che/\_cache.py} with toolchain + feature flags.
    \item \texttt{./waf plane} --- builds plane vehicle target into \codepath{build/sitl/bin/arduplane}.
    \item \texttt{./waf --targets tests/test\_math} --- builds a single gtest binary into \codepath{build/sitl/tests/test\_math}.
  \end{itemize}
  \vfill
  The \texttt{--debug} flag preserves debug symbols; without it, gdb is much less useful and L1 will be harder than necessary.
  \vfill
  \textbf{Never} run \texttt{waf} with \texttt{sudo} (file ownership in \codepath{build/} will then bite you on the next non-sudo build).
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{wscript is small}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item \citerange{ArduPlane/wscript}{1}{40} --- vehicle \texttt{wscript}; declares vehicle-specific build options + the libraries this vehicle pulls in.
  \end{itemize}
  \vfill
  Each library directory has its own \texttt{wscript} describing its sources. The top-level \texttt{wscript} orchestrates the lot.
  \vfill
  Conceptually identical to a CMake target list, just expressed in Python.
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{\texttt{AP\_<FEATURE>\_ENABLED} --- what drags in what}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item \citerange{Tools/scripts/build\_options.py}{1}{50} --- file header + first few \texttt{Feature(...)} entries.
  \end{itemize}
  \vfill
  When you read code and see \texttt{\#if AP\_TERRAIN\_AVAILABLE} or \texttt{\#if HAL\_QUADPLANE\_ENABLED}, that flag is in \codepath{build\_options.py}.
  \vfill
  \textbf{Hard rule the codebase observes:} a core, non-optional component must never depend on an optional one. The base build must succeed when optional features are disabled.
  \vfill
  This is the rule that makes extraction tractable. We return to it in M10.
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{The file you will extract first --- preview}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item \citerange{libraries/AP\_L1\_Control/AP\_L1\_Control.cpp}{1}{15} --- three lines define the contract with the rest of the codebase.
  \end{itemize}
  \vfill
  \begin{itemize}
    \item \texttt{\#include <AP\_HAL/AP\_HAL.h>} --- the umbrella include.
    \item \texttt{extern const AP\_HAL::HAL\& hal;} --- the singleton handle.
    \item Opening of \texttt{var\_info[]} --- the parameter table.
  \end{itemize}
  \vfill
  Engineer 1's capstone (M11) starts from exactly this file. M10 walks the extraction recipe end-to-end.
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Reference docs --- consult, don't narrate}

\section{Module M4 --- HAL Architecture (3.0 h, internals)}

\begin{frame}{Module M4 --- HAL Architecture with adoption-seam framing}
  \textbf{3.0 h, lecture+code-walk+lab, \emph{internals} depth.}\\[1ex]
  HAL is the \emph{primary} extraction seam. Every adoption discussion later in the course returns to this module's framing.
  \vfill
  Learning objectives:
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item Explain why HAL exists: same flight code on STM32 (ChibiOS), Linux, SITL, ESP32.
    \item Read \texttt{class AP\_HAL::HAL}: enumerate the 20-ish subsystems it owns.
    \item Trace one HAL call end-to-end: \texttt{Plane::ahrs\_update} $\rightarrow$ AHRS $\rightarrow$ IMU $\rightarrow$ SITL backend.
    \item Recognise the four canonical access patterns: \texttt{extern const AP\_HAL::HAL\& hal;}, \texttt{AP\_HAL::millis()}, \texttt{AP\_HAL::micros()}, \texttt{hal.scheduler->delay(...)}.
    \item Read one \texttt{hwdef.dat} (CubeBlack) at the 15-min adoption side-bar.
  \end{enumerate}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{The class in one paragraph}
  \texttt{AP\_HAL::HAL} is a struct of pointers to subsystem driver objects.
  \begin{itemize}
    \item \citerange{libraries/AP\_HAL/HAL.h}{21}{30} --- class opening + constructor signature start.
    \item \citerange{libraries/AP\_HAL/HAL.h}{35}{90} --- full constructor parameter list: 10 UARTs, I\textsuperscript{2}C, SPI, WSPI, AnalogIn, Storage, console, GPIO, RCInput, RCOutput, Scheduler, Util, Flash.
  \end{itemize}
  \vfill
  One concrete instance is constructed per build. For SITL: \citerange{libraries/AP\_HAL\_SITL/HAL\_SITL\_Class.cpp}{1}{80}. For ChibiOS hardware: the analogue under \codepath{libraries/AP\_HAL\_ChibiOS/}.
  \vfill
  Every consumer reaches the singleton through \texttt{extern const AP\_HAL::HAL\& hal;}.
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}[allowframebreaks]{The four free functions every adopter needs}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item \citerange{libraries/AP\_HAL/system.h}{14}{21} --- \texttt{micros16}, \texttt{micros}, \texttt{millis}, \texttt{millis16}, \texttt{micros64}, \texttt{millis64}.
  \end{itemize}
  \vfill
  \texttt{AP\_HAL::millis()}: ms since boot, \texttt{uint32\_t}, \textbf{wraps every $\sim$49 days}.\\
  \texttt{AP\_HAL::micros()}: $\mu$s since boot, \texttt{uint32\_t}, \textbf{wraps every $\sim$71 minutes}.
  \vfill
  ArduPilot code consistently uses the subtract-and-let-it-wrap idiom: \texttt{(now - last) * 1.0e-6f}. The 64-bit variants exist for paths that need wall-clock-relative comparisons without wrap concerns.
  \instfaq{Why expose 16-bit variants?}{Footprint --- some embedded paths can use \texttt{uint16\_t} to keep a struct in cache or in a single register. Read at survey here; M5 returns to the scheduler at code level.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}[fragile]{Trace target --- \texttt{Plane::ahrs\_update}}
  The first \texttt{FAST\_TASK} in the scheduler table runs every fast-loop tick.
  \begin{itemize}
    \item \citerange{ArduPlane/Plane.cpp}{165}{200} --- function body.
  \end{itemize}
  \vfill
  Body is short:
  \begin{Verbatim}[fontsize=\small,frame=single]
void Plane::ahrs_update()
{
    arming.update_soft_armed();
    ahrs.update();
#if HAL_LOGGING_ENABLED
    if (should_log(MASK_LOG_IMU)) {
        AP::ins().Write_IMU();
    }
#endif
    roll_limit_cd  = aparm.roll_limit*100;
    pitch_limit_min = aparm.pitch_limit_min;
    ...
}
  \end{Verbatim}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{What \texttt{ahrs.update()} actually does}
  One line drives the full estimator stack on each tick.
  \vfill
  Drop sequence:
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item \texttt{AP\_AHRS::update} (the frontend).
    \item Active EKF backend (\texttt{NavEKF3} by default for plane).
    \item IMU read in \texttt{AP\_InertialSensor}.
    \item Platform-specific IMU driver:
    \begin{itemize}
      \item On SITL: synthetic data from the physics model in \codepath{libraries/SITL/}.
      \item On hardware: I\textsuperscript{2}C/SPI sensor read.
    \end{itemize}
  \end{enumerate}
  \vfill
  M7 reads the EKF half of this stack at code level. M6 reads the sensor frontend/backend.
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}[fragile]{The umbrella include}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item \citerange{libraries/AP\_HAL/AP\_HAL.h}{1}{31} --- the umbrella header.
  \end{itemize}
  \vfill
  Every consumer of HAL writes:
  \begin{Verbatim}[fontsize=\small,frame=single]
#include <AP_HAL/AP_HAL.h>
extern const AP_HAL::HAL& hal;
  \end{Verbatim}
  \vfill
  That header pulls in the full HAL surface plus the \texttt{extern} declaration.
  \vfill
  The two lines together are the contract a client signs with the platform abstraction.
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Adoption side-bar --- what comes with \texttt{AP\_HAL}}
  \adoptionsidebar{
  \textbf{What this subsystem buys you}: a clean, idiomatic abstraction over time, scheduler delay, UART, storage, and bus drivers.\\[0.5ex]
  \textbf{What comes with it}: the full \texttt{AP\_HAL::HAL} constructor surface ($\sim$20 subsystems), \texttt{extern const AP\_HAL::HAL\& hal;} reaching everywhere, and \texttt{AP\_HAL::millis()}/\texttt{micros()} free functions.\\[0.5ex]
  \textbf{Realistic adoption pattern}: provide a stub \texttt{AP\_HAL::HAL} against your platform's native APIs. Minimum viable surface for an algorithm-only extraction = \texttt{millis} + \texttt{micros} + a single \texttt{Scheduler::delay()}.\\[0.5ex]
  \textbf{Cost to keep vs replace}: keeping ArduPilot's HAL means owning a wide compatibility surface forever. Replacing it (thin shim) means owning a narrow one --- but you must update it any time your extracted algorithm starts touching a new HAL function.}
\end{frame}

  Directives to recognise: \texttt{MCU}, \texttt{OSCILLATOR\_HZ}, \texttt{SERIAL\_ORDER}, \texttt{SPIDEV}, \texttt{IMU}, \texttt{BARO}, \texttt{COMPASS}, \texttt{AIRSPEED}.
  \vfill
  A new board is, to first approximation, a new \texttt{hwdef.dat}. Treated as an adoption side-bar here because your stack's primary interest is extracting algorithms into your own HAL, not maintaining ArduPilot as the host runtime.
  \instadvanced{The 5-day source has a full standalone module on board porting (\siblingplane\ M12). Folded in here as a side-bar for this audience.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Compare to your stack --- HAL width}
  Most flight stacks have a HAL boundary. The interesting comparison is \emph{width}.
  \vfill
  \begin{itemize}
    \item ArduPilot's HAL is wide ($\sim$20 subsystems).
    \item Wide HALs make \emph{porting a board} easier (the contract is exhaustive).
    \item Wide HALs make a \emph{minimum-viable extraction} harder (you have to know which subsystems your target subsystem actually touches).
  \end{itemize}
  \vfill
  In M10 we walk a minimum-viable mock HAL for \texttt{AP\_L1\_Control} --- it needs only \texttt{AP\_HAL::micros()} plus a degenerate scheduler.
\end{frame}
