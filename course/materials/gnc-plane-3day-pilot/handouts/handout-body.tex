\section*{The framing this audience needs}

You will compare every ArduPilot decision to your own stack. The course casts ArduPilot's choices as \textbf{one design among many}, not as the correct answer. We call out trade-offs explicitly:

\begin{itemize}
  \item The parameter system (\texttt{AP\_Param} + \texttt{AP\_GROUPINFO}) is highly flexible and self-documenting, but pays a per-access lookup cost (linear scan over \texttt{var\_info[]}).
  \item The singleton pattern (\texttt{AP::scheduler()}, \texttt{AP::ins()}, etc.) is convenient but creates static-init ordering hazards and makes test isolation harder.
  \item The cooperative scheduler is fast and predictable, but a 50 ms task overrun is silent --- there is no pre-emptive watchdog above the task layer.
  \item The HAL is the cleanest extraction seam in the codebase, but it is wide ($\sim$20 subsystems). A minimum viable adoption HAL is a small fraction of the surface, walked in M4 and M10.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Math-as-code, not math-on-slides.} The math is in the code; we read the code.

\paragraph{The \texttt{errorScore} formula --- read aloud.}
Score is gated by \texttt{tiltAlignComplete \&\& yawAlignComplete}. Inside the gate, \texttt{MAX}-folded over four channels:
\begin{itemize}
  \item GPS: \texttt{0.5f * (velTestRatio + posTestRatio)} --- always evaluated.
  \item Altimeter: \texttt{hgtTestRatio} --- unit weight.
  \item Airspeed: \texttt{0.3f * tasTestRatio}, gated on \texttt{assume\_zero\_sideslip()}, $\geq$ 2 sensors, \texttt{EKF\_AFFINITY\_ARSP}.
  \item Magnetometer: \texttt{0.3f * (mag.x + .y + .z)}, gated on \texttt{EKF\_AFFINITY\_MAG}.
\end{itemize}
The 0.3 factor on airspeed and mag is a deliberate sensitivity reduction --- gust-driven airspeed innovations would otherwise force false lane switches in cruise. The \texttt{EK3\_AFFINITY} parameter selects which observation channels participate.

\textbf{What \texttt{AP\_NavEKF3} buys you}: a multi-lane 24-state EKF with mag-yaw alignment, wind-state, airspeed/optical-flow/range-finder fusion, and a defensible lane-arbitration policy.\\

\paragraph{TECS energy split.} Total specific energy $STE = h + V^2/(2g)$; energy balance $SEB = h - V^2/(2g)$. Throttle commanded against $STE$; pitch commanded against weighted blend of $STE$ and $SEB$. Blend weight is \texttt{TECS\_SPDWEIGHT} (default 1.0 = 50/50 split).

\paragraph{L1 lateral acceleration.} Damping factor \texttt{0.3183099 = 1/$\pi$}. \texttt{\_L1\_dist = MAX(1/$\pi$ $\cdot$ damping $\cdot$ period $\cdot$ groundSpeed, dist\_min)}.

\paragraph{Roll PID scaler.} \texttt{scaler $= (V_{ref} / V_{current})^2$} --- makes the controller behave consistently across the airspeed envelope without re-tuning.

\section*{Acronyms}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{HAL} --- Hardware Abstraction Layer.
  \item \textbf{SITL} --- Software In The Loop.
  \item \textbf{EKF} --- Extended Kalman Filter (state estimator).
  \item \textbf{AHRS} --- Attitude and Heading Reference System (the abstraction; EKF implements it).
  \item \textbf{TECS} --- Total Energy Control System (combined altitude + airspeed controller).
  \item \textbf{L1} --- Lateral path-following controller (Park, Deyst, How 2004).
  \item \textbf{TECS, L1} --- the two flagship controllers extracted in the capstone.
  \item \textbf{IMU} --- Inertial Measurement Unit.
  \item \textbf{GCS} --- Ground Control Station (MAVProxy in this course).
  \item \textbf{MAVLink} --- the binary message protocol.
  \item \textbf{EAS / TAS} --- Equivalent / True Air Speed.
  \item \textbf{NVM} --- Non-Volatile Memory (EEPROM + SD on real boards; flat file in SITL).
  \item \textbf{DAL} --- Data Access Layer (the indirection between EKF and sensor frontends).
  \item \textbf{Waf} --- ArduPilot's Python-based build system.
\end{itemize}
