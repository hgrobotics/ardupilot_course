\section*{Acronyms}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{PID} --- Proportional-Integral-Derivative controller. ArduPilot's standard feedback-control building block.
  \item \textbf{EKF} --- Extended Kalman Filter. The state estimator. Black box for this course.
  \item \textbf{IMU} --- Inertial Measurement Unit. Accelerometers + gyroscopes.
  \item \textbf{GCS} --- Ground Control Station. \textsc{MAVProxy} in this course (text only).
  \item \textbf{MAVLink} --- the binary message protocol between autopilot and GCS.
  \item \textbf{MAV\_CMD} --- a MAVLink command identifier (e.g. \texttt{MAV\_CMD\_NAV\_WAYPOINT}).
  \item \textbf{RTL} --- Return To Launch.
  \item \textbf{AHRS} --- Attitude and Heading Reference System (the abstraction; EKF implements it).
  \item \textbf{SITL} --- Software In The Loop. Same binary, on your laptop.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Audience}
First-year aerospace engineering undergraduates. High-school physics in hand; basic calculus in progress. \textbf{No} prior controls theory, embedded systems, Linux fluency, or programming. In-person, TA-supported computer lab. The autopilot runs in SITL on student laptops --- no real flight, no bench hardware.

\section{Day 1 --- Set up SITL, first flight, flight modes, parameters}

\subsection*{Day 1 goal}
By the end of Day 1, every student has SITL on their own laptop, can launch a simulated quadcopter, can arm / take off / hover / switch mode / land / disarm using MAVProxy commands, and can change a parameter and observe a behaviour change.

\subsection{Module 1.1 --- What is an autopilot, ArduPilot, SITL? (30 min, \emph{survey})}

An autopilot is the software that decides, hundreds of times per second, what each motor on the vehicle should do. ArduPilot is one of the two large open-source autopilot stacks (the other is PX4). It targets small unmanned vehicles and supports many vehicle types out of one source tree.

\textbf{Key handoff:} the same ArduPilot code we read together also runs unchanged on a real flight controller. The difference between a Pixhawk and your laptop is only the HAL backend: \codepath{AP\_HAL\_ChibiOS} for STM32, \codepath{AP\_HAL\_SITL} for the simulator, \codepath{AP\_HAL\_Linux}, \codepath{AP\_HAL\_ESP32}.

\textbf{Two pieces of context to carry forward.} ArduPilot is safety-critical software --- the code we read flies real vehicles, so every change must be correct, tested, and reviewable by a human maintainer. That posture is why the codebase looks the way it does: verbose names, compile-time feature flags (\codepath{\#if AP\_<FEATURE>\_ENABLED}), and a hardware abstraction layer between autopilot and silicon. The architecture is three layers: the six vehicle directories at the top level, a large \codepath{libraries/} tree of sensor drivers and controllers, and the HAL with its four backends listed above.

\textbf{Code-reading} (open and look only at what the heading line tells you):
\begin{itemize}
  \item \citeline{ArduCopter/Copter.h}{181} --- one line: \texttt{class Copter : public AP\_Vehicle \{}. The file you will return to all course.
\end{itemize}

\instnote{Module 1.1 is half a slide on landscape and three short cites. Resist deepening into release cadence or governance.}
\insttiming{30 min budget. Demo at the end is $\sim$5 min --- tight.}

\subsection{Module 1.2 --- Set up your laptop: install, build, launch (1 h, lab, \emph{applied})}

\textbf{Two anchors that show what \texttt{-v ArduCopter} does:}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \citeline{Tools/autotest/sim_vehicle.py}{287} --- the literal string \texttt{'ArduCopter.elf',}.
  \item \citerange{Tools/autotest/sim_vehicle.py}{1073}{1085} --- option-parser block for \texttt{-v}/\texttt{--vehicle} and \texttt{-f}/\texttt{--frame}.

\textbf{Lab L1 --- ``First SITL launch.''}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \texttt{Tools/environment\_install/install-prereqs-ubuntu.sh} (Ubuntu).
  \item \texttt{git clone --recurse-submodules https://github.com/ArduPilot/ardupilot.git \&\& cd ardupilot}
  \item \texttt{./waf configure --board sitl} \quad then \quad \texttt{./waf copter}
  \item \texttt{Tools/autotest/sim\_vehicle.py -v ArduCopter --console --map}
\end{enumerate}
\textbf{Pass criterion:} a \texttt{HEARTBEAT} arrives at the GCS, the map renders the vehicle, the console prints \texttt{STATUSTEXT: APM:Copter \dots} and \texttt{online system 1} within $\sim$30 s.

\textbf{If apt cascades errors across unrelated packages on a fresh Ubuntu:} the configured mirror is almost always stale, not the script. Raise your hand --- the TA will diagnose with \texttt{apt-cache madison <pkg>} and switch \codepath{/etc/apt/sources.list.d/ubuntu.sources} to \codepath{archive.ubuntu.com} if the configured mirror is behind.

\textbf{The MAVProxy commands used in this course} (and only these):
\begin{itemize}
  \item \texttt{mode <NAME>}, \texttt{arm throttle}, \texttt{rc 3 1700},
  \item \texttt{param show <NAME>}, \texttt{param set <NAME> <VALUE>}.
\end{itemize}

\instfaq{Build fails on a fresh Ubuntu 26.04 with cascading apt errors.}{Stale mirror, not the script. Verify with \texttt{apt-cache madison <pkg>} vs \texttt{archive.ubuntu.com}, then switch the mirror.}

\subsection{Module 1.3 --- Your first flight: arm, take off, land, disarm (45 min, lab, \emph{applied})}

\textbf{The mode you start in: STABILIZE.} Levels you when sticks released. Throttle goes straight through to motor thrust. \emph{No} altitude or position hold. The closest copter mode to manual flight.

\textbf{Code-reading:}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \citerange{ArduCopter/mode.h}{77}{109} --- master enum: \flmode{STABILIZE} = 0, \flmode{ALT\_HOLD} = 2, \flmode{LAND} = 9. Recognise the shape, do not memorise.
  \item \citerange{ArduCopter/mode_stabilize.cpp}{9}{64} --- \texttt{ModeStabilize::run}. Read the comment: \emph{``stabilize\_run --- runs the main stabilize controller; should be called at 100hz or more.''} The body is a small state machine; the comment is enough for survey depth.
\end{itemize}
\instnote{Do not unpack the spool-state \texttt{switch} in \texttt{ModeStabilize::run}. Survey depth.}

\textbf{Lab L2 --- ``First flight (STAB $\rightarrow$ LAND).''}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \texttt{mode STABILIZE}, \texttt{arm throttle}, \texttt{rc 3 1700}.
  \item Wait altitude $> 10$~m.
  \item \texttt{mode LAND}, wait \texttt{Disarming motors}.
\end{enumerate}
\textbf{Pass criterion:} \texttt{Disarming motors} statustext within 90 s of \texttt{arm throttle}.

\subsection{Module 1.4 --- Flight modes from a pilot's view (45 min, \emph{survey})}

\textbf{Three-bucket taxonomy} (verbatim --- use this, not the historical \texttt{mode.h} order):
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Manual} --- \flmode{STABILIZE}, \flmode{ACRO}. Sticks $\approx$ motor outputs. Letting go $\Rightarrow$ vehicle drifts.
  \item \textbf{Stabilized-with-altitude} --- \flmode{ALT\_HOLD}. Autopilot holds altitude; pilot still flies horizontally.
  \item \textbf{Autonomous} --- \flmode{AUTO}, \flmode{GUIDED}, \flmode{RTL}, \flmode{LAND}. Autopilot decides.
\end{itemize}
\flmode{LOITER} is intentionally \emph{not} on this course's mode tour.

\textbf{init / run pattern} (shared by every mode):
\begin{itemize}
  \item \citerange{ArduCopter/mode_althold.cpp}{9}{22} --- \texttt{ModeAltHold::init}: ``initialise althold controller.'' Called once on entry. Hands speed/accel limits to its controller.
  \item \citerange{ArduCopter/mode_althold.cpp}{26}{104} --- \texttt{ModeAltHold::run}. Survey: state-machine names \texttt{MotorStopped}, \texttt{Landed\_Pre\_Takeoff}, \texttt{Takeoff}, \texttt{Flying}. Each branch decides differently what to do with the pilot's stick.
\end{itemize}
\instnote{Do not unpack the algebra inside any branch of \texttt{ModeAltHold::run}. Survey depth; redirect to the GNC course if a student starts solving.}

\textbf{Mode switching can be refused:}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \citerange{ArduCopter/mode.cpp}{313}{396} --- \texttt{Copter::set\_mode}.
  \item \citeline{ArduCopter/mode.cpp}{394} --- \texttt{mode\_change\_failed(\dots, "requires position");}
  \item \citeline{ArduCopter/mode.cpp}{404} --- \texttt{mode\_change\_failed(\dots, "need alt estimate");}
\end{itemize}
The autopilot prefers to refuse than to be wrong.

\textbf{In-module demo:} \texttt{param set SIM\_GPS\_DISABLE 1; mode RTL} $\rightarrow$ refused. Then \texttt{param set SIM\_GPS\_DISABLE 0} to restore.

\instadvanced{\flmode{LOITER}, \flmode{POSHOLD}, and the position-hold family are covered in detail by \siblingplane\ Day 2 and \siblingquadplane\ Day 3.}

\subsection{Module 1.5 --- Parameters (30 min, \emph{applied})}

A parameter is a named, persistent runtime-configurable value. \textbf{Not} compiled in --- read from non-volatile storage at boot.

\textbf{The \texttt{@Param} annotation block:}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \citerange{ArduCopter/Parameters.cpp}{44}{51} --- the eight lines that declare \texttt{PILOT\_THR\_FILT}: seven \texttt{// @...} comment lines plus \texttt{GSCALAR(...)}. Recognise: \texttt{@Param} (16-char name cap), \texttt{@DisplayName}, \texttt{@Description}, \texttt{@Range}/\texttt{@Values}, \texttt{@Units}, \texttt{@User}.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{A real parameter table:}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \citerange{ArduCopter/Parameters.cpp}{33}{67} --- start of \texttt{Copter::var\_info[]}: \param{FORMAT\_VERSION}, \param{PILOT\_THR\_FILT}, \param{PILOT\_THR\_BHV}, \param{GCS\_PID\_MASK}. Recognise the \texttt{GSCALAR(\dots)} macro.
  \item \citerange{ArduCopter/Parameters.cpp}{149}{191} --- \param{FLTMODE1}--\param{FLTMODE6} and \param{FLTMODE\_CH}. PWM band $\rightarrow$ flight-mode number.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{In-module hands-on:}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \texttt{param show SIM\_WIND\_SPD} (default 0).
  \item \texttt{param set SIM\_WIND\_SPD 5} --- watch hover wobble.
  \item \texttt{param set SIM\_WIND\_SPD 0} --- restore.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Day 2 --- Under the hood: sensors, scheduler, data path, failsafes, closing lab}

\subsection*{Day 2 goal}
By the end of Day 2, you can read (not write) the call path from sensor reading $\rightarrow$ scheduler tick $\rightarrow$ mode \texttt{run()} $\rightarrow$ PID $\rightarrow$ motor output, name what the EKF is for without solving any equations, and have run a SITL flight in which an injected GPS failure triggered the EKF failsafe.

\subsection{Module 2.1 --- Sensors and the scheduler (45 min, \emph{survey})}

\textbf{Sensors, one sentence each:}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{IMU} --- accel + gyro. Sampled fastest.
  \item \textbf{Barometer} --- pressure $\Rightarrow$ altitude.
  \item \textbf{Magnetometer} --- field $\Rightarrow$ heading.
  \item \textbf{GPS} --- global position + ground velocity.
\end{itemize}
The \emph{estimator} fuses these into one trusted answer for ``where am I, how fast am I going, which way am I pointing.'' Estimator $=$ EKF. Black box here.

\textbf{The scheduler is one list, run forever:}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \citerange{ArduCopter/Copter.cpp}{113}{149} --- \texttt{Copter::scheduler\_tasks[]} opening. \texttt{FAST\_TASK} every loop, \texttt{SCHED\_TASK} rate-limited.
  \item \citeline{ArduCopter/Copter.cpp}{117} --- \texttt{FAST\_TASK(run\_rate\_controller\_main),}.
  \item \citeline{ArduCopter/Copter.cpp}{126} --- comment \texttt{// run EKF state estimator (expensive)}.
  \item \citeline{ArduCopter/Copter.cpp}{127} --- \texttt{FAST\_TASK(read\_AHRS),} (this runs the EKF).
  \item \citerange{ArduCopter/Copter.cpp}{151}{201} --- selected \texttt{SCHED\_TASK} entries. Different rates for different freshness.
  \item \citeline{ArduCopter/Copter.cpp}{201} --- \texttt{SCHED\_TASK(ekf\_check, 10, 75, 84),} (10~Hz).
\end{itemize}

\textbf{One line of \texttt{main()}:} \citeline{ArduCopter/Copter.cpp}{998} --- \texttt{AP\_HAL\_MAIN\_CALLBACKS(\&copter);}. Macro at \citerange{libraries/AP_HAL/AP_HAL_Main.h}{35}{41}.

\textbf{The main loop:} \citerange{libraries/AP_Vehicle/AP_Vehicle.cpp}{558}{566}. Line 561: \texttt{scheduler.loop();}. The autopilot's main loop does literally one thing.

\textbf{Hands-on:} \texttt{param show SCHED\_LOOP\_RATE} $\rightarrow$ 400~Hz. Optionally \texttt{grep -c SCHED\_TASK ArduCopter/Copter.cpp}.

\subsection{Module 2.2 --- From stick input to motor output: the data path (45 min, \emph{survey} + 1 \emph{applied})}

\textbf{Memorise this sentence:}
\begin{quote}
  Stick $\rightarrow$ mode \texttt{run()} $\rightarrow$ attitude controller $\rightarrow$ rate controller $\rightarrow$ PID $\rightarrow$ motor mixer $\rightarrow$ ESC.
\end{quote}

\textbf{Stage 3 (the only \emph{applied} cite):}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \citeline{ArduCopter/Copter.cpp}{117} --- the \texttt{FAST\_TASK} entry.
  \item \citerange{ArduCopter/Attitude.cpp}{10}{24} --- \texttt{run\_rate\_controller\_main}: sets \texttt{dt}, calls \texttt{rate\_controller\_run()}.
  \item \citerange{libraries/AC_AttitudeControl/AC_AttitudeControl_Multi.cpp}{457}{485} --- \texttt{rate\_controller\_run\_dt}. Three PIDs, one per axis:
  \begin{itemize}
    \item \citeline{libraries/AC_AttitudeControl/AC_AttitudeControl_Multi.cpp}{473} --- roll.
    \item \citeline{libraries/AC_AttitudeControl/AC_AttitudeControl_Multi.cpp}{476} --- pitch.
    \item \citeline{libraries/AC_AttitudeControl/AC_AttitudeControl_Multi.cpp}{479} --- yaw.
  \end{itemize}
  Inputs: target angular velocity, gyro reading, \texttt{dt}.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Stage 4 --- PID, as a black box:} \citerange{libraries/AC_PID/AC_PID.cpp}{196}{272} --- \texttt{AC\_PID::update\_all}. Identify \texttt{float P\_out = (\_error * \_kp);} and stop. PID is ``compute an error, multiply by P, add the integral, add the derivative, output.''

\textbf{Stage 5 --- motor mixer:} \citerange{libraries/AP_Motors/AP_MotorsMatrix.cpp}{213}{244} --- \texttt{output\_armed\_stabilizing} opening. Takes 4 numbers; decides how hard each of 4 motors pushes.

\textbf{Why the X-frame angles:} \citerange{libraries/AP_Motors/AP_MotorsMatrix.cpp}{592}{602} --- the \texttt{MOTOR\_FRAME\_TYPE\_X} branch. Motors at $+45^\circ, -135^\circ, -45^\circ, +135^\circ$, alternating CCW/CW. The CCW/CW alternation cancels prop torque; that is what lets a quadrotor yaw at all.

\subsection{Module 2.3 --- Why does the autopilot ever decide for itself? Failsafes (45 min, \emph{applied})}

\textbf{Four failsafes:} RC loss, battery, GCS link loss, \textbf{EKF variance} (the closing lab triggers this one).

\textbf{The EKF failsafe code path:}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \citerange{ArduCopter/ekf_check.cpp}{30}{90} --- \texttt{Copter::ekf\_check} (10~Hz).
  \item Names: \texttt{ekf\_check\_state.fail\_count}, \texttt{ekf\_check\_state.bad\_variance}, \texttt{g.fs\_ekf\_thresh} ($=$ \param{FS\_EKF\_THRESH}), \texttt{EKF\_CHECK\_ITERATIONS\_MAX}.
  \item \citeline{ArduCopter/ekf_check.cpp}{83} --- \texttt{LOGGER\_WRITE\_ERROR(EKFCHECK, BAD\_VARIANCE);} (the dataflash row to read out).
  \item \citeline{ArduCopter/ekf_check.cpp}{86} --- \texttt{gcs().send\_text(MAV\_SEVERITY\_CRITICAL, "EKF variance: \%s", \dots);} (the live statustext).
  \item \citeline{ArduCopter/ekf_check.cpp}{89} --- \texttt{failsafe\_ekf\_event();} (the action).
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Pre-arm checks --- same posture before take-off:} \citerange{ArduCopter/AP_Arming_Copter.cpp}{8}{20}. \texttt{run\_pre\_arm\_checks} starts with ``exit immediately if already armed.'' Same code-base personality: refuse than be wrong.

\subsection{Module 2.4 --- Closing lab: scripted flight + forced EKF failsafe (1.25 h, \emph{applied})}

\textbf{Reference fingerprint:} \citerange{ArduCopter/ekf_check.cpp}{79}{89} --- the inner block that fires when \texttt{fail\_count >= EKF\_CHECK\_ITERATIONS\_MAX}.

\textbf{Step A --- clean run ($\sim$25 min):}
\begin{verbatim}
  arm throttle
  mode GUIDED
  takeoff 30
  (hover 30 s)
  mode RTL
  (wait disarm)
\end{verbatim}
Pass: disarmed within 180 s; no \texttt{CRITICAL} statustext.

\textbf{Step B --- failsafe injection ($\sim$50 min):} same prefix, then at t = 60 s after \texttt{arm throttle}:
\begin{verbatim}
  param set SIM_GPS_DISABLE 1
\end{verbatim}
Pass criteria (all four required):
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \texttt{STATUSTEXT} severity \texttt{CRITICAL}, text starts \texttt{EKF variance:}, within 30 s of injection.
  \item Mode change to \flmode{LAND} or \flmode{RTL} within 30 s of injection.
  \item \texttt{Disarming motors} within 240 s of original arm.
  \item Dataflash \texttt{ERR} row with \texttt{Subsys = EKFCHECK}, \texttt{ECode = BAD\_VARIANCE} (read via \texttt{mavlogdump.py --types ERR}).
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Deliverable} (record on paper or in a text file):
\begin{enumerate}
  \item The MAVProxy statustext at the moment of failure.
  \item The final mode the vehicle was in at disarm.
  \item The dataflash \texttt{ERR} row.
\end{enumerate}
